UK CBAM 2027: what importers need to do, and how it differs from the EU

The UK Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism starts on 1 January 2027. It looks like EU CBAM, but the threshold, the scope and the way you pay are different. If you buy from producers who also sell into the EU, you can collect one set of data and use it for both.

The short answer

From 1 January 2027, the liable person for imports of aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen, and iron and steel goods into the UK pays CBAM if their imports reach the £50,000 threshold. Registration opens on 1 January 2028, with a deadline of 31 January 2028 for the first year. The first accounting period is the whole of 2027, and the first return and payment are due by 31 May 2028. After that, accounting periods are quarterly.

The charge is based on the direct emissions embodied in the goods, including relevant precursors. You can use verified actual emissions from the producer, or default values set by the government. As of HMRC's records guidance updated on 3 November 2026, the UK default values had not yet been published.

The importer workflow, step by step

  1. Check your commodity codes. Compare the 8-digit commodity code of every import line against the UK CBAM goods list.
  2. Check that you are the liable person. HMRC has guidance on who counts as the importer for CBAM. Agree it with your customs agent.
  3. Monitor the threshold every month. On the first day of each month from 1 January 2027, check whether you imported £50,000 or more of CBAM goods in the previous 12 months. You also need to register if you expect to import £50,000 or more in the next 30 days.
  4. Ask producers for data now. Verified actual emissions are optional, but you need the producer's verification report to use them. Without it, you use the default value.
  5. Keep records from day one. Even if you end up below the threshold, you need records to show you are not liable.
  6. Register by 31 January 2028. For the 2027 period, through HMRC's service, which opens on 1 January 2028.
  7. File and pay by 31 May 2028. The return for 1 January to 31 December 2027. Then file quarterly, by the last working day of the month after each quarter.
  8. Claim carbon price relief where it applies. If a carbon price was paid abroad, you can claim relief with verified data and a carbon pricing verification form. Relief cannot exceed the CBAM liability.

Records to keep from 1 January 2027

One data set for two regimes

If your producers sell into both markets, the installation data they hold is largely the same. The difference is in what each regime counts and how it is reported. Store the producer's data once, per installation and per good, with direct, indirect and precursor emissions kept separately. Then generate the UK and EU views from it.

  • For the UK: direct and precursor emissions, net mass in kilograms, value for the threshold test, quarterly from 2028.
  • For the EU: add indirect emissions for cement and fertilisers, track net mass in tonnes against the 50-tonne threshold, and produce the annual declaration.
